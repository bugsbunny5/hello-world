Report Analysis Target

Overall, the report wishes to analyze COVID19 data from a global perspective and a US perspective.

The first question of interest is how the US death rate per confirmed case compares to other countries from around the globe.

The second question of interest is the dynamic of death rates per capita and COVID cases per capita across the US. The US data allows the extra analysis since the underlying data contains population information. My theory is the more cases there are per capita, the more overwhelmed the health system will be, causing more deaths per capita.

The final question area of interest is are state per capita COVID death rates in 2021 predictive of 2022.

Importing Data

  • The data is from Johns Hopkins Github site.
  • This data set deals with COVID19.
    • A call is made out to the website to read the csv file of data in
    • All data sets are imported.
  • The data sets include confirmed cases and deaths, as well as US and global information. The data sets are:
    • COVID19 cases confirmed in the US.
    • COVID19 cases confirmed globally.
    • COVID19 deaths in the US.
    • COVID19 deaths globally.
  • Data commentary overall:
    • In general the US data has more information and detail.
    • The US data has FIPS code, the administration/city area, and province/state .
    • The US death data has population as well.
    • The global information does not have population. Countries are listed, but only select countries have province/state filled.
    • Both data sets contain daily observations from 01/22/2020 to 03/09/2023

  select(-c(UID, iso2, iso3, code3, FIPS, Admin2, Lat, Long_, Combined_Key)) %>%

  group_by(State, Country) %>%
  summarise(across(everything(), sum, .names = "{.col}"), .groups = "drop") %>%
  pivot_longer(cols = -c(State, Country), names_to = "date", values_to = "cases") %>%
  mutate(Year = year(mdy(date))) %>%
  group_by(State, Country, Year) %>%
  summarise(tot_cases = sum(cases), .groups = "drop")

Report Conclusion

The overall analysis showed interesting outcomes from the COVID data analysis, on a global level and within the US.

The first question of how death rates per cases compared across the global showed Mexico had high death rates among larger countries. Further the UK had high numbers relative to peer in the first year, but then their death rates came down. The US death rates per case seem to be in line with peer countries.

The second question of interest was the dynamic of death rates per capita and COVID cases per capita across the US. The data showed as case rates rose across states, death rates typically followed. This showed the potential for deaths to increase as the health system gets overwhelmed.

The final question surrounded was State within the US a good predictor of death rates. From the base analysis the answer is yes. Several more remote states had lower rates and several more metropolitan states had higher death rates. This seems to suggest social distancing ability impacts death rates.

Bias introspection

I am native to the United States and had preconceived notions that state’s experienced different COVID death rates. While modeling showed using state as a predictor is strong, the model was overfit. I came to a conclusion this is fine since of the strong GINI metric, but the bias is the conclusion agreed with my apriori.

There is also potential general bias within the data. COVID saw different standards of record keeping with COVID. Different states and different countries sometimes only kept good numbers on deaths vs cases, especially once the pandemic went longer and some people didn’t report symptoms. Further, earlier on in the pandemic some people died before rigorous testing occurred. There was also politics in terms of outbreak numbers, causing some political pressure in record keeping.
